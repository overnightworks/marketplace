# Terms duties

The rules for standard terms (Allgemeine Geschäftsbedingungen), each in a few
words with its paragraph and a link to the official text; the wording that
binds is the official text, read as `SKILL.md` Reading the law says. Terms an
app sets for every user are standard terms in the sense of BGB § 305(1). Most
rules decide whether a clause binds at all, so the check asks of each clause
whether it survives them, and of the app whether the terms become part of the
contract.

## Edition read

- BGB ([gesetze-im-internet.de][bgb-305]): `xml.zip`, Stand
  "zuletzt geändert durch Art. 6 G v. 23.7.2026 I Nr. 226"; every paragraph
  page linked below answered 200.

## Incorporation

| Duty | Law | Check |
|---|---|---|
| The terms bind only if the user is expressly referred to them at conclusion, can take note of them reasonably, and agrees | [BGB § 305(2)][bgb-305] | app code: where the user signs up or first uses the service, the reference and the agreement are shown; a gap is the app's, not the text's |
| In electronic commerce, the terms can be retrieved and stored in reproducible form at conclusion | [BGB § 312i(1) sentence 1 Nr. 4][bgb-312i] | composed page and app code; applicability from the audit register |
| An individual agreement prevails over the terms | [BGB § 305b][bgb-305b] | nothing to write |

## Wording

| Duty | Law | Check |
|---|---|---|
| A clause so unusual that the user need not expect it does not become part of the contract | [BGB § 305c(1)][bgb-305c] | every clause the app adds is one a user of this service expects, or is said where it cannot be missed |
| Doubt in interpretation goes against the user of the terms | [BGB § 305c(2)][bgb-305c] | each clause has one reading |
| A clause that is not clear and understandable can itself be an unreasonable disadvantage | [BGB § 307(1) sentence 2][bgb-307] | the minimality pass; plain words, no cross-reference chains |
| Content control covers only clauses that deviate from or add to the law; other clauses can fail only on transparency | [BGB § 307(3)][bgb-307] | a clause that only restates the law is cut |
| A failed clause leaves the rest of the contract valid, and the law fills the gap | [BGB § 306(1), (2)][bgb-306] | a severability clause adds nothing the statute does not say: cut it |

## Content

| Duty | Law | Check |
|---|---|---|
| No unreasonable disadvantage against good faith; presumed where a clause departs from an essential idea of the law or endangers the contract's purpose | [BGB § 307(1), (2)][bgb-307] | every clause the app adds |
| Clause bans with room for judgement, among them changing the promised service (Nr. 4), deemed declarations (Nr. 5), deemed receipt (Nr. 6), unavailability of the service (Nr. 8) | [BGB § 308][bgb-308] | a clause on changing the service or the terms, or on notices, is checked against the number it touches |
| Clause bans without room for judgement, among them liability for injury to life, body or health or for gross fault (Nr. 7), other liability exclusions (Nr. 8), the term of a continuing contract (Nr. 9), burden of proof (Nr. 12), form of notices (Nr. 13) | [BGB § 309][bgb-309] | every liability clause keeps Nr. 7; a subscription or term keeps Nr. 9 |

## Who the contract partner is

| Duty | Law | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Towards an entrepreneur, § 305(2) and (3), § 308 Nr. 1 and 2 to 9 and § 309 do not apply; § 307 still does | [BGB § 310(1)][bgb-310] | who the users are comes from the audit register |
| Towards a consumer, terms count as set by the entrepreneur, terms used once are controlled too, and the circumstances of conclusion count | [BGB § 310(3)][bgb-310] | the default when the register names consumers or is silent |
| Not for inheritance, family and company law or collective labour agreements | [BGB § 310(4)][bgb-310] | `n/a` for an app's terms unless the inventory says otherwise |
